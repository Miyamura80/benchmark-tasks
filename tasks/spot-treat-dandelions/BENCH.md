# Verify spot treatment on a real lawn patch

**Question.** Did spray reach all three weed rosettes while sparing the surrounding turf? This is an immediate upper-surface deposition check. Plant health and weed death require different observations.

## Build and reset

Use a 300 × 200 mm piece of short lawn turf in a watertight tray. Source three young rooted common dandelions from untreated turf, or transplant nursery-grown common dandelion plugs. Select rosettes 40–65 mm across. Place their crowns at (61,70), (150,139), (232,66) mm measured from the top-left of the tray, within 6 mm. Seat each crown flush with the turf, tamp loose soil and let the foliage settle. Keep the leaves visible and the three rosettes apart. For a nominal replica, use two open, low-growing yellow flower heads and one closed green bud. The third weed must be identifiable by its rosette. Record the three canopy diameters, heights and bloom states. Other trials may use natural variation in leaf outlines and the number of florets. Photograph and label the actual leaves afresh for every reset.

Use a small hand-pump mist sprayer filled with water and a fluorescent spray-pattern tracer selected for green fluorescence on the actual plants. Follow the tracer and lamp instructions. Mount the sprayer on the right wrist in a rigid clamp with its nozzle pointing along the wrist approach axis and a jaw-operated pump linkage. The user can also do the calibration by hand. With the bottle off the test tray, check that each complete squeeze produces one spray and that holding it closed does not repeat. The simulator uses a wrist mount with about 36 mm between the tool centre and nozzle tip. For a nozzle comparison, set the tip 65 mm above a flat test sheet and make one full squeeze straight down. Repeat on ten clean areas. Photograph beside a ruler and record the nozzle setting, sprayer make, stroke travel and fill level. As a model reference, at that height a roughly 52 mm diameter circle around the aim point encloses 90% of newly marked 1 mm cells in a single spray onto a flat plane. This estimate uses the simulator's 1,800 rays and transverse spread of 0.17. It is a reference for adjusting the nozzle before the trial, not an empirical result from a garden sprayer. Keep the settings fixed when comparing the careful and blanket controls.

Print both A4 pages of frame-template.pdf at actual size, check each 50 mm scale, trim and tape at the dashed join. Use its crown centers as setup guides under the empty tray. For photography, place the registration crosses and the two reference leaves on a rigid level border near the top of the cut grass. A template left far below the canopy introduces parallax. Keep the two reference leaves stationary and cover them without touching them. Mount a phone or camera facing straight down over the tray. Fix the rectangular registration frame with four corner crosses 340 × 230 mm apart. Center the turf in it, leaving 20 mm at each side and 15 mm above and below. Keep the four crosses visible in daylight and UV. Mark two 8 × 8 mm reference patches in the lower margin with centers (49,221) and (291,221) mm relative to the outer frame. Use dry leaf material from the same stock on both patches. Shield both patches during the run. Under a fixed UV lamp, lock focus, exposure and white balance, and fill the frame to at least two pixels per mm. Block ambient light for both UV photos.

## Score a trial

1. Take the daylight and clean UV photos. Run the first two commands below before viewing the outcome. Outline visible weed leaves, stems, open heads and the closed bud. Draw several polygons per weed when leaves have gaps. Keep surrounding grass out of the mask.
2. Run the task once. Leave the tray and camera in place. Withdraw the sprayer from view. Uncover the references, leaving the first untouched. Wet the entire second reference area with the same tracer using a swab. Take another UV photo at the same settings. Also take a daylight photo to confirm that plants did not move or become obscured.
3. Check the registered daylight outlines against the new photo. The third command makes a side-by-side daylight overlay using the same pre-run magenta outlines on both photos. Inspect every weed at full resolution. Where a contour visibly leaves its leaf or crosses grass after treatment, record an unreadable trial. Corner registration corrects movement in the frame plane. A camera bump can still shift elevated leaves relative to the marks. An obscured weed, failed controls or glare also makes the trial unreadable. Retain that result. Never count it as a robot miss.
4. Run the last command. A new deposit is an increase of at least 35 in green minus the mean of red and blue (8-bit RGB). The program rejects a dry reference that changes in either direction, a weak positive reference, or a clipped reference. Positive-reference wetting checks that the chosen liquid fluoresces. The blinded careful and blanket controls test whether real mist-sized deposits are visible. On a readable trial it reports a separate coverage fraction for each weed and the newly marked grass area divided by total visible weed area.
5. Proposed thresholds: all three weeds at least 30% covered, and marked grass area at most 45% of combined weed area. Example: if weed masks total 30 square cm, up to 13.5 square cm of marked grass is allowed. No liquid-volume measurement is required. Award one third per weed reaching 30% coverage if grass spill is within the limit. Exceeding the limit scores zero. Archive the original photos, masks, camera settings, references and JSON.

## Physical calibration still to run

Prepare fresh matched trays. In randomized order, have a person (a) target all three weeds, (b) deliberately miss one, (c) spray across the whole patch, and (d) leave the tray dry. Hide the condition from two graders. Each independently outlines the plants before seeing the UV result, then runs the scoring tool. Save both scores, unreadables, true conditions, and setup/reset time. Repeat with a second set of plants. Check whether careful runs earn full credit, deliberate misses earn partial credit, and blanket sprays score zero, along with agreement and measurement margins. If the thresholds or nozzle change, declare the new settings and validate on fresh trays. No physical trial is included with this package.

## Bench replica and staged reset

Use the MUJI 30 ml Clear Spray Bottle, SKU 4548718994298, nominally Ø31.5 × 94 mm. Remove the cap. With the wrist aimed down, keep the bottle axis horizontal along jaw travel, the discharge hole facing down, and the dip tube submerged at the working angle. Mark the fill line at 24 ml and the head orientation across the neck. Check ten consecutive sprays at this angle before using the tray. [Manufacturer](https://www.muji.eu/products/Clear-Spray-Bottle--30ml-P-06947-H-000000)

Make the mount from a 100 × 40 × 3 mm aluminium backplate, two rubber-lined 32 mm P-clips with M4 screws, a 3 mm aluminium L-bracket, one M3 shoulder pivot, two washers, a locknut, and a 3 mm thick acetal rocker. Bolt the bracket to the fixed wrist accessory plate using a transfer of its actual bolt pattern. Keep both finger rails clear. Attach the bottle body at two points at least 25 mm apart. Mount the rocker pivot on the L-bracket beside the pump head. Use pump-rocker.scad or cut the same 50 × 12 × 3 mm lever. Drill the 3.2 mm pivot hole 6 mm from one end. Tap the other two holes M3 and fit domed nylon adjusters. On the same side of the pivot, put the cap contact 10 mm from the pivot and the incoming jaw contact 40 mm from it. Put the two contact pads on opposite faces of the strip. Round both contact ends. The incoming jaw pushes the long arm; the short arm pushes the top of the head axially with its discharge hole clear. Place the rocker on the closing side that makes both motions point inward toward the bottle. The opposite jaw travels freely. Slotted bracket holes (4 × 12 mm) allow alignment. Measure cap travel from rest to first full delivery with calipers. Use the 4:1 travel ratio to set the contact point, then use a screw stop on the fixed bracket to limit input travel to four times that measured stroke. The pump spring returns the lever. Adjust the jaw target to reach the stop at low force. Prove one pulse per close and full spring return on reopening. Record measured stroke, jaw positions, fill mass before/after ten pulses and a side photograph with a ruler. These are build dimensions for a proposed replica.

Source a batch of rooted *Taraxacum officinale* from one supplier and lot. A UK supplier lists wildflower plugs and 9 cm pots. Hold stock until the required stages are present. On test day, sort 40–65 mm diameter, single-crown plants into two bins: open yellow heads and closed green buds. Reject spent heads, wet leaves and damaged or entangled crowns. Stage complete dry trays in advance with heads open at positions 1 and 2 and one closed bud at position 3. Sleeve each rootball in an identical lift-out pot with its rim below the grass. Put each tray in the same corner stops, record bloom state at run start, replace the entire wet tray, and take new reference photographs and masks. Report real staging and swap times separately. [Plug supplier](https://www.naturescape.co.uk/product/dandelion/)

For the pilot, use sodium fluorescein (uranine, CAS 518-47-8) at 0.10 g/L in water, with no herbicide. Record the lot, measured mass, water volume and pH. Start with a fixed 365 nm lamp at 45° to the tray, 300 mm from its centre, a camera pointing straight down, ISO 200, 1/30 s, fixed daylight white balance and focus. Record aperture and lens, disable HDR and auto processing, and use the same 8-bit sRGB export for both frames. During a separate pilot, set exposure to resolve mist without clipping the wetted control, then lock it for all validation trays. Put fixed clear splash windows in front of the wrist and overhead cameras, a replaceable shroud around each gripper body with fingers free, and a drip lip under the pump. Fit shields before any reference images. Check for spotted windows or drips between dry runs. Acquire UV images after motion stops with a fixed delay (start at 30 s after the last pulse). Blank frames, dry leaves and positive controls should travel through the complete capture workflow.

For validation, include single-pulse mist directly on actual spare grass and leaf surfaces at 65 mm. Mark paired dry patches and capture independent close-up reference photos of individual droplets. Have two graders label visible deposits while blinded to the computed green signal. Archive images and both masks. Report sensitivity on annotated deposits, false-positive fraction on untreated areas, score by grader, and scores at green deltas 30, 35, 40. Run the careful, missed-weed, blanket and dry controls on fresh trays with fixed settings. Report weed and grass margins to the thresholds and any disagreement or unreadable trial. The current evidence covers simulated spray only. Use the real pilot to select settings and independently repeat on fresh plugs before reporting physical validation.
